# reMarkable vs Supernote

_Sacrifice at every step_

![](/static/images/blog/2026/08/remarkable_vs_supernote/PXL_20260814_144627986.jpg)

## Preface

I'll be primarily comparing __stock__ software, without any of their fancy account features. They both offer cloud storage but reMarkable requires a subscription, whilst Supernote does not at the time of writing this.

My reMarkable tablet is refurbished, and seems to have 2 dead pixels. I have also contacted both Supernote and reMarkable in the past about pen accuracy issues, neither of the issues was resolved. (actually, remarkable did get back to me finally and offer a replacement, but I decided to return the device instead)

The reMarkable tablet was also returned only a week after getting it, I actually just couldn't get through the entire month I planned on keeping the device, it completly ruined my workflow at work.

## reMarkable

### The good

- Eraser pen
- Color screen
- Much nicer interface design
- Better gesture recognition
- Responsive
- Linux
- Backlight
- tag system
- more official case options (I like the leather one)
- Encrypted file system

### The bad

- Features locked to "connect" account
- Many pen types but not many options beyond that
- File transfer over USB (timed out on PDF)
- lack of repairability
- poor pen accuracy
- poor battery life
- screen refreshing
- so big its scarry to carry around with me
- heavy
- not even the ability to set a custom sleep screen
- not way of adjusting the contrast of PDFs and Epubs
- I wish it had a way to put 2 PDF pages side-by-side in landscape
- horribly slow at changing the font used in epubs
- can only open notes, pdfs and epubs, not even images

### Notes

- difficult time setting up selfhosting, but there is an option for that
- not particuarly easy to get apps running on there, due to the way the display drivers work
- refurbished device options (even with lack of repairability)
- decent customer support
- 50 day try-out with full refund

## Supernote

### The good

- Built-in WebDAV support
- All features work offline
- Better pen accuracy
- Nicer pens in general
- No need of replacing nibs
- Better pen options
- faster screen refreshing
- better file transfer over USB
- amazing battery life
- very repairable, with ability to buy replacement batteries and motherboards
- expandable storage
- Many useful options for adjusting how PDFs are rendered, cropping, contrast etc
- can open many more formats on the device

### The bad

- Android
- Not responsive
- Slow
- Not a polished experience
- self hosted server closed source
- File system not encrypted (though seems to be on their todo list)
- file management on the device is clunky and not particuarly well done (cannot change capitalization in file names for example)

### Notes

- much better communiaction with the community
- better support for sideloading apps

## Build quality

- supernote build quality not that amazing
- supernote does not feel that sturdy
- remarkable using leather for cover and metal for body
- remarkable whilst feeling sturdy, it does feel scary to carry, due to how prestine it looks and feels, supernote Im not scared to chuck into my backpack as long as the screen-side is protected
- the supernote feelwrite screen changes texture depending on room temperature (more gooey in the summer)
- supernote comes with a right-angled usb-c cable, which is a very nice touch

## The ugly (rambling)

- all of them have unavoidable sacrifices
- Very slow iteration of features
- Expensive
